The Husband Speaks

Pray Fate not interrupt the one we are
Who seeks the light of life within thee, Heart,
For in this yet unborn, we stand afar—
A bridge from here beyond, no more to part.

Through this that comes, we will be one alive
In eons yet to be, nor earth forego;
Linked yet to men, though parting must arrive,
We're deathless still, for this is self we sow.

Guard then thou well, preserving fast our key
To way of future, linked through us to past,
That from thee, heart, ere long these issues free—
Our conscious love, the infant we have cast.

Loose too, thy hopes, but give them voice that I
May share thy wish, thy dreams of love to be;
To bless our oneness, and our life thereby;
Repledging—heart, unending love for thee.

Lift Your Eyes

Now lift your eyes from greenery
Of shining ornamented tree:
Above the tinsel, colored light,
There's deeper meaning in the night!

Think, if you will, of that first day,
The shepherds, angels come to pray:
Kings of Orient wandered far
To place gifts under brilliant star,
At feet of God's most precious One,
The Baby, Saviour, His own Son.

While Mary, mother, woman blessed,
With heartfelt joy held God's Bequest,
And angel voices tidings spread
In wondrous song, relieving dread—
That world should know at last the birth
Of Saviour to men on earth!

Of Joseph, who was there to state
The census, and through heavenly fate
Should raise Him, boyhood unto man,
We wonder, did he understand?
Or could he sense the sacrifice?
Of Him God sent to bring us life!

Yes, lift your eyes from greenery,
To hill beyond, and three-branched tree—
The piercing, wretched end in view,
For Him who gave his life for you:
That last your soul may climb the height!
It's His birth to recall tonight!

Out of Earth

Do you love life? Then you must dream as I,
For dreams provide an ascent for the morrow,
As life provides the substance for a dream.

What matters caste, with happiness about?
A pleasure in the task and faith in self,
And deep within an unintruded peace.

Can you, outreaching, barely touch the sky,
Or feel its close approach in years ahead,
Without belief your best is yet to come?

We've life and dreams that closely intertwine,
Each, in the time that yet has not arrived,
And without fail shall be with breaking dawn.

Turn loose the bonds on one within that's you,
For dreams are yielding—cushion loss, regret,
Remorse; a potent wellspring of our hopes.

Balance life and dreams, that unconquered heights
Appear not so forbidding to the view,
Waiting only your assault tomorrow!

Anticipation

Pray fate not interrupt the one we are,
Who seeks the light of life within thee, heart!
For in this yet unborn, we stand afar—
A bridge from here beyond, no more to part.

Through this that comes, we will be one alive
In eons yet to be, nor earth forego—
Linked yet to men, though parting must arrive,
We're deathless still, for this is self we sow!

Guard then thou well, preserving fast our key
Of way to future, linked through us to past,
That from thee, heart, ere long there issues free
Our conscious love, the infant we have cast.

Loose too, thy hopes, but give them voice that I
May share thy wish, thy dreams of what's to be;
To bless our oneness, and our life thereby—
Repledging, heart, unending love for thee!

Mother

Albuquerque, New Mexico · September 1963

I think of mother when there's gentle sun,
I find her too in rain on thirsty lea;
She's part of rest, when last the day is done,
And sings in brook from mountain to the sea.

For she is loving warmth and tenderness,
And holds the cup to succor pain of thirst;
She brings our minds in peace to evening rest,
And joyfully she places others first.

Mother, stand near,
As you have in the past:
Know you are dear,
To the day I see last.

I, too, in living would be as you art,
Loving, sympathetic, with quiet grace,
Compassionate, with understanding heart,
Through you, I feel I've touched the Saviour's face.

Lady of the Night

Albuquerque, New Mexico · September 1963

From you, oh lady of the night,
Who watches clouds gray and white
Drift languorously past,
And features no race nor caste,
But in benevolence shines
Upon all of mankind's
Whines and prejudices,
We should take a lesson.

To this effect:
Whate'er the creed or sect
Of all those that we know,
Rich or poor, high or low,
Race or religious faith:
We will give heed to each
With true equality.
