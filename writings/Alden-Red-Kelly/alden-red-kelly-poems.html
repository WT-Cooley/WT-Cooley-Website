Sanctuary

February 9, 1976
Were I to pass this way again, to be
A man on earth, and sorely need a mate,
And if my choice were yet completely free,
It's you I'd have, nor passion yet abate
Despite the years, now six and thirty gone,
Our bridge, from youth and pair to being one.

So full the years, the press of early need
For labor? What then found my hands for wage?
To me you left the choice of work, the deed
To occupy my part of world's stage.
This was far best the way, you early knew,—
Fulfilled, as man, in toil I'd long pursue.

There Is Beyond

June 23, 1943
We are not lumps of dross having no soul,
Nor yet the slate, which, selected from coal,
Is thrown unknowing with other leavings.

Are we then goal-less, fearing what we face?
An unknown future, cringing from the mace
Thrust out by hate, fear, and private grievings!

We have a strength! The logic in our minds
Enhanced by something higher, which seeks and finds
The reason why, and helps us through sorrows.

Like the year—wherein each season will bring
What follows, after winter's cold comes spring!
Shall we then say there will be no tomorrows?

Seize the Day

August 11, 1943
I killed a day, and now my sorrow feel,
Attempting to recall what's gone for e'er.
Those moments past now bear a timeless seal,
The strength of which I just now came aware.

Too often, prone are we, to let time's pace
Speed on without a personal advance—
Perhaps don't start, so don't attempt the race,
Or hit a rut and blame one else, perchance.

This I would say, since I did lose my way
In winged time, and yet do feel my loss,
Behooves it you to fully seize the day!
The minutes used are jewels, those wasted—dross!

This Singing World

List to harmonic voices
That sing from out the earth.
The birds call mates, their choices,
Give heartfelt songs of birth!

The winds that sing and carry
Kind nature's perfume far,
To listening ears sound merry,
While clouds sing past a star.

Green leaves dancing in the breeze,
Stems and blades a-quiver,
Reflect singing of the trees—
Echoed from the river.

Nature sings a song of life,
Awake and gaily fair,
Songs of happiness that's rife.
Are you of songs aware?

New Mexico

July 1945
Thou, rugged land of vision carried far,
Begat by nature, lying close to death
In bearing such a one. Yet, shine thy star,
For truly finding thee I scarce have breath!

Thy ageless sands display a master's care,
Warm yellow, fearless red, and brilliant white,
So vividly disclosed for men to share
In light of day; a glorious, blinding sight!

Yet, soft thy nights, outpouring gorgeous blue,
To bless and cherish those within thy arms;
Those diamond lamps, set rare in heavenly hue,
And moonlight sheen to more enhance thy charms.

Arroyo

July 1945
I slash the earth
with steep sharp banks,
boldly cutting where I please.

I grow larger
with each season,
gouging weak earth as I go.

You can't pen me!
I seize the pen
and swallow to earthen guts.

I, arroyo,
remain long here
after race or man has gone!

Halt my progress?
Arrest me now?
I'm Arroyo! Eternal!

Peanut Butter Buster

A lively adventure by Alden W. Kelly

Peanut Butter Buster is a children’s story about a tiny person who comes alive when a jar of peanut butter is left open and unstirred. After climbing from the jar, Buster slips on the oil, hides beneath a paper towel, and escapes when Mother discovers him.

He runs into Sonny and Sister, avoids Tabby the kitten, and encounters Fido, Robert the robin, and Jocko the blue jay. The surviving manuscript follows Buster as he searches for his fortune and longs for a peanut-butter brother. Draft markings and uncertain wording have been omitted here rather than silently presented as finished prose.
