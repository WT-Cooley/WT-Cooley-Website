In Hope Is Strength

AW. KELLY - FEB.22, 1944

      Thou strength of Hope, that fills
      The future’s radiant glow,
      Our help on present hills,
      Who smooths the rapids flow:

      Aid earthy to aspire
      With minds unfettered, calm,
      yet glowing inner fire
      to earn the victor's palm.

      For those who suffer, - give
      Thy strong support and cheer:
      Bestow thy will to live,
      Protect from unknown fear.

      Exalt the children's sight
      To verify the truth,
      That outlook must be bright,
      Our living not uncouth.

      These strengths I ask for men,
      When e'er the need arise
      For birth of hope again,
      Within a soul that dies!

New Mexico

July 1945
      Thou, rugged land of vision carried far,
      Begat by nature, lying close to death
      In bearing such a one. Yet, shine thy star,
      For truly finding thee I scarce have breath!

      Thy ageless sands display a master's care,
      Warm yellow, fearless red, and brilliant white,
      So vividly disclosed for men to share
      In light of day; a glorious, blinding sight!

      Yet, soft thy nights, outpouring gorgeous blue,
      To bless and cherish those within thy arms;
      Those diamond lamps, set rare in heavenly hue,
      And moonlight sheen to more enhance thy charms.

      How rugged! Yes, with thy arroyos deep,
      In slashing, paintul scars across thy breast;
      And there sharp rising heights where mesas keep
      Eternal watch of mountains crowning crest.

      Keep silent guard as I, poor mortal thing,
      Grope cautiously in hope within thy realm;
      Imbue thy strength and steadfastness to bring
      This human ship to port with true-steered helm!

Arroyo

July 1945
      I slash the earth
          with steep sharp banks,
              boldly cutting where I please.

      I grow larger
          with each season,
              gouging weak earth as I go.

      You can't pen me!
          I seize the pen
              and swallow to earthen guts.

      Yes, land I mark
          and scar. I know
              my power; lay not hand on me!

      Man, frail man, stay;
          Come not to me.
              I am earthy, ages old.

      I, arroyo,
          remain long here
              after race or man has gone!

      Halt my progress?
          Arrest me now?
              I'm Arroyo! Eternal!

Mesa

by A. W. Kelly. – July 1945 
      Look you, mortal passing by,
      Gaze your till upon my steeps,
      Pointed straightly to the sky.
      Think you we're but earthen heaps?

      Cast your ogling eyes around,
      At my flattened top, my base
      Wide spreading, as I look down
      To consider human race.

      Short lived man, were you on earth
      When the water flooded here?
      Since that time we've seen your birth,
      Saw you hairy, dumb and queer!

      We have seen you kind demand
      a life from the unkind range,
      Saw you grovel in the sand,
      Build your cities, make a change.

      Coronado's spanish file
      Searching treasures, silver, gold,
      Wandered through our land awhile
      As he sought in venture bold.

      Desperados, men of steel;
      There were cattle barons too,
      All these saw me, Mesa; real;
      I their deaths and passing knew!

      Look on me, then wend your way,
      Through your little work and space,
      While I here quiescent lay,
      With the eons keeping pace.

Anticipation

AWK., Nov. 26, 1945

      Let fate not interrupt the one we are,
      Who seeks the light of day within thy womb,
      For in that yet unborn we stand afar, -
      In ages hence lie deathless in the tomb!

      That we by love may thus be interspersed
      Through eons yet to come, nor earth forego,-
      Again to live! - the deathly ties dispersed;
      To die, how then? our blood in this we sow.

      Yet guard thou well, that we lose not our key
      Of way to future, linked through us to past,
      That from thyself ere long there issues free
      A being wholly us, whom we have cast!

      Dream too thy hopes, in silence it desire,
      Or give thy wish a voice that I may feel
      Thy conscious longing and the mental fire,
      What fears bespeak?- or venture the ideal.

      To trust, to believe, and fright at naught to come,
      Whate'er appears in thoughtful imagery;
      Face truth not phantoms dark, take comfort from
      Thy place, to stand as rugged base of tree!

"Our Love is Young"

by Alden W. Kelly [~1944]

      Our love is young, - o'er stress of age that hues
      And hides to other eyes the youth we were;
      How young our love? - nor icy winds can lose
      Tremulous lips, - the passion that you stir.

      Dear heart of mine, that was and is fore'er,
      Climactic need of man from birth to end,
      My own, thou art the one my soul finds fair,
      Our love is young, no need therein pretend.

      If else it were then young to me, my heart,
      I would alas, be perforce then unborn!
      When love's not young, far better ban the start;
      Keep young our love to greet the blessed morn!

      Submitted by Alden W. Kelly
      3305 Campus Blvd.
      Albuquerque, N.M.

In Future, Hope!

AWK [to Evie when she was feeling our move to New Mexico was almost too much ~1944]

      Still broad horizons doom which menace not,
      Nor poor appear from our sequestered spot.

      Rise to the hope of challenge yet away,
      In timorous heart bid courage welcome, stay!

      Hold proud thy head; to stronger future cast
      A tie, a prayer, nor linger in the past.

      Let thy memory recall but pleasantness,-
      Blight not what comes, but softly lend caress;

      That thou and I though future seem unclear,
      May pass therein; have each, - and so no fear!

Dark Night of Fear

AWK. [November 7 1945]

      Dark night who finds the orphan cold,
      Nor restful place therein for him,
      Abate the ills, in comfort fold
      The mortal's wish though lighted dim,
      That he may savor good.

      What then dark night, great fearful beast,
      Who lags to leap in peril strange;
      Allay such fears, provide the least
      Harshness for them, let terrors change,
      To bring the calm you could.

      So night of ill and dark despite
      Provide an ebon haven, turn
      To bring at end of day respite;
      The relaxation that men earn
      Is theirs, dispel it not.

      This night let fall protective hope
      Which succors all and rests the heart,
      So men refreshed may next day cope
      With world's affairs, nor lose their part.
      Give faith, - improve their lot!

Out of Earth

-Alden w. Kelly [~1950s]

      Do you love Life? Then you must dream as I,
      For dreams provide and ascent for the morrow.
      As life provides the substance for a dream.

      What matters caste, with happiness about?
      A pleasure in the task and faith in self,
      And deep within an unintruded peace.

      Can you out reaching, barely touch the sky,
      Or feel its close approach in years ahead,
      Without belief your best is yet to come?

      We’ve life and dreams that closely inter-twine
      Each, in the time that yet has not arrived,
      And without fail shall be with breaking dawn.

      Turn loose the bonds on one within that's you,
      For dreams are yielding, -cushion loss, regret.
      Remorse; -A potent wellspring of our hopes.

      Balance life and dreams, that unconquered heights
      Appear not so forbidding to the view,
      Waiting only your assault tomorrow!

Let The Spirit Enter

Alden W. Kelly [1950s]

      Sacramental moment, inspire my thoughts again
      Relief from earth's intrusion; sins we have as men,
      That I may soar, relate to you, and through your son
      Realize the promise, eternal life He won.

      The grace of His presence, personified in bread,
      Redemption; saving cross, we are no longer dead.
      His body sacrificed, on high for all mankind,
      The path is marked, three nails; His love we all can find.

      Mainstream of life, the blood - to quench all believers thirst,
      That Father, through His Son, redeemed us who were, cursed,
      And by the word of Him, He sent to prove His love
      Remember our Lord's death, til He comes from above:

      Press in then, to the table, - Bread and wine today
      Reach out your hands, cleansed and pure, striving for the way
      His son pictured as described in Apostle's word,
      By sacrament through faith, eternal life assured!

Lift Your Eyes

(22 December, 1955)

      Now Lift your eyes from greenery
      Of shining, ornamented tree;
      Above the tinsel, colored light
      There's deeper meaning in the night!

      Think, if you will, of that first day,
      The shepherds, angels come to pray,
      Kings of Orient wandered far,
      To place gifts under signal star.
      At feet of God’s most precious One,
      The Baby, Saviour, His own Son.

      While Mary, mother, woman blest
      With heartfelt Joy held God's Bequest
      And Angel voices tidings spread
      With wondrous song, relieving dread,
      That all at last should know the birth
      Of Saviour to men on earth.

      Ot Joseph, who was there to state,
      The census, and through heavenly fate
      Should raise Him boyhood unto man.
      We wonder, did he understand?
      Or could he sense the sacrifice
      Of Him God sent to bring us life?

      Yes, lift your eyes from greenery
      To hill beyond, and three branched tree, 
      The piercing, wretched end in view
      For Him who gave His life for you:
      That last your soul may climb the height.
      It's his birth to recall tonight!

      A. W. Kelly
      Elder, Monte VistaChristian Church

The Husband Speaks

[~1959]

      Pray Fate not interrupt the one we are
      Who seeks the light of life within thee,
      Heart’
      For in this yet unborn, we stand afar
      A bridge from here beyond - - no more to
      part

      Through this that comes, we will be one 
      alive
      In eons yet to be, nor earth forego
      Linked yet to men, though parting must 
      arrive
      We're deathless still, for this is self 
      we sow

      Guard then thou well - - preserving fast 
      our key
      to way of future, linked through us to 
      past
      That from thee heart, ere long these issues
      free
      Our conscious love, the infant we have
      cast

      Loose too, thy hopes- - but give them voice
      that I
      May share thy wish, thy dreams of love to
      be
      To bless our oneness, and our life thereby;
      Repledging - - - Heart unending love for
      thee.
