    • The Parting

    Published in Wisconsin paper following his father’s death, January 1940
    
      Outside, the calm of humid summer day,
      Within, the strain of parting. Loving twain;
      That day his last, she trying to be gay—
      To let him know her loss was freedom's gain.

      Could she exist without this man? Her own,
      More closely bound then merely earthly ties-
      Her life with him, - blessings of heaven blown 
      Essence, of something far beyond the skies!

      He was a man. You say he could not feel
      The loss in leaving her whom he adored?
      Marriage! Her love! Their life which was so real
      Now put behind him. Down tyranny! Up sword!

      Then he was gone and she found quick release,
      As every woman does at such an hour.
      The flood of tears brought emotional surcease,
      And from a higher strength came quiet power.

    Parental Problems

    BY A. W. KELLY, AUG. 10, 1943 
          A flood of joy, so easily perceived,
      In every motion, word and happy glance.
      Her thrill of life scarcely can be believed.
      So fully things and moments help enhance
      Existence here. she doesn't feel deceived.
      She barks at dogs and pulls up mother's plants.
      Then too, sometimes she has to be relieved
      Of knives or forks, yet takes an adult's grants
      Gratefully, without being too aggrieved
      Apparently. She takes an awkward stance
      still, while walking, a worse can't be conceived,
      Yet it's effective, - makes her daddy dance
      At playtime. Then eats - finally she's heaved
      In bed and parents come out of their trance
      until next morning.

    "Seize The Day"

    by Alden W. Kelly Aug. 11, 1943
    
      I lost a day, and now my sorrow feel,
      Attempting to recall what's gone for e'er.
      Those moments past now bear & timeless seal,
      The strength of which I just now came aware.

      Too often, prone are we, to let time's pace
      Speed on without a personal advance.-
      Perhaps don't start, so don't attempt the race,
      Or hit & rut and blame one else, perchance.

      This I would say, since I did lose my way
      In winged time, and yet do feel my loss,
      Behooves it you to fully seize the day!
      The minutes used are jewels, those wasted - dross!

    "Our Due"

    Dedicated to those husbands and fathers who will soon be called. Alden W. Kelly – December 1941
    
      Within a realm of trees and liquid lines
      That mark the edge and surface of a lake,
      We made our home and knew all nature's signs,
      Yet more than our just due we would not take.

      Was our due just? the love we bore for each,
      The life we knew, - ourselves had built by hand.
      Wordless nature!we heard the words she'd preach.
      For devotees she had no reprimand.

      Yes, life was good, though wealth came not our way.
      We knew no envy, our riches well we saw -
      Our friends, Our God, our baby too one day,
      Our private world was surely without flaw.

      Then, - the drummings of vast catastrophe.
      The steady throbbing tread of marching men.
      Once more the call to arms to keep us free,
      America needs her sons yet again.

      And what of us? our country asks its due
      To overcome the ones who demand all.
      The army needs its men, each ship a crew.
      We'll all help build an axis wailing wall!

    There Is Beyond

    June 23, 1943
          We are not lumps of dross having no soul,
      Nor yet the slate, which, selected from coal,
      Is thrown unknowing with other leavings.

      Are we then goal-less, fearing what we face?
      An unknown future, cringing from the mace
      Thrust out by hate, fear, and private grievings!

      We have a strength! The logic in our minds
      Enhanced by something higher, which seeks and finds
      The reason why, and helps us through sorrows.

      Like the year—wherein each season will bring
      What follows, after winter's cold comes spring!
      Shall we then say there will be no tomorrows?

      We all have suffered loss, which looming large,
      Makes us forget we bear a sacred charge -
      Each one of us must look beyond the present.

      Each one of us must look beyond the present,
      Consider not those yet unborn a wraith
      A cloud, obscure: within preserve this faith --
      For a greater good humanity was meant.

    This Singing World

    AW Kelly – August 10, 1943
    
      List to harmonic voices
      That sing from out the earth.
      The birds call mates, their choices,
      Give heartfelt songs of birth!

      The winds that sing and carry
      Kind nature's perfume far,
      To listening ears sound merry,
      While clouds sing past a star.

      Green leaves dancing in the breeze,
      Stems and blades a-quiver,
      Reflect singing of the trees—
      Echoed from the river.

      Nature sings a song of life,
      Awake and gaily fair,
      Songs of happiness that's rife.
      Are you of songs aware?

    "We Soar Afar"

    Alden W. Kelly – August 9, 1943
    
      Within, release - no earthly bonds or ties
      Save only those we wish to yet retain.
      We leap to heights above this world's skies,
      Our wishful soul doth freedom sure obtain.

      How quickly then one leaves the commonplace
      To join the winds as they whisk through the clouds.
      Without the earth, how rapid is the pace,-
      Mountains far below, wrapped up in their shrouds.

      No feeling there of friendliness or hurt,
      Only the calm swift moving world of sky.
      Just out of reach the dark beyond is girt
      With stars, whose beauty for men n'er will die.

      Small wonder then, we seek escape within!
      Imagination surely cuts our chains.
      We're shackle-free to dream what might have been,-
      Then back to earth, subconscious thinking wanes.

    "Where Lies The Strength?"

    Alden W. Kelly – 1943
    
      Do you still hold to this as truth?
      To think of self and no one else,
      Uplift no mankfor fear uncouth
      Stay not whip which another welts?

      Strong belief in self-sufficiency,
      Help not, though called upon for aid, -
      Like "new order" efficiency,
      Desire the workers poorly paid?

      Suppressing, grinding into dirt
      Those who luckless hap! to be
      Consider not the feelings hurt
      If you smear those who don't agree?

      Or otherwise in daily life,
      Expect the others down to bow?
      This, then your fate - a constant strife,
      And death will sneer at ugly brow!

    "To Wisconsin – A Toast"

    Alden W. Kelly – 1943
    
      Oh, mother state, thou seem'st tender to me,
      However much the stranger's tales decry
      Thy heat and cold, and beauties do not see.-
      They ope not eyes to charms thy looks imply.

      Thy hills and heights, from which one sees afar
      O'er thy lakes and trees, bays and long headlands,
      Perchance to naked rock that leaves a scar,-
      A bluff, unsculptored save by sacred hands.

      Lush forests with the contrast of white birch
      And green leaves, flaunting blades upon the air.
      There, spreading farms, with here a cross-roads church,
      Thou, state of mine, with natural beauty rare.

      Once more the thoughts of meadows, streams, and pine:
      Straight growing, ageless as outcropping stone,
      All these contribute to thy beauteous lines,
      Oh, mother state, these make you stand alone!

    "So Let Me Build!"

    [~pre-1944]
    
      The urge to build inherent is in men
      A wish to form from old a something new;
      To reconstruct the shape of what has been,
      Or else with words a newer truth imbue.

      Yet time is lived by people not aware
      They daily build an edifice erect,
      That weathers storms if only formed with care
      Of flawless things, which truly are select.

      Foundation then, which best of all is stone:-
      The solid truth,a man to honor bound,
      Responsible, a man to trust alone,
      God-fearing, with a moral tone that's sound.

      Permit the light to enter every nook
      By windows clear and wide, which give a view
      Unclouded by bias and dark outlook.
      Let not a shade be-dim bright inward hue.

      A door that's stout, yet withal freely swings, 
      A guard from thieves of sense, - protective, strong;
      Which eases way for he who knowledge brings,
      And cloisters not within a living song.

      O'er all a roof secure, withstanding wrath
      And elements, affected not by fate
      Which weakens those who choose a lesser path.
      Thus, simply built, are lives considered great!

    Make True The Course

    [~pre-1944]
    
      The course was set one day when all was calm,
      No waves too high nor sun that burned too deep.
      On minor hurts we used curative balm;
      Though easy trod, we thought the hills too steep.

      That Day the road and goal, how simply seen:
      To school to higher arts, then wed, and work
      The span we may:- so exit from the scene,
      Replaced by others come from out the murk.

      How now we blindly hunt the way we knew,
      Our vision short, scarred hands, a bleeding heart
      Still hope express, though never aid in view.
      So, grimly hold, nor higher aim depart.

      Some day at last, a secure peace shall raise
      Her tender hand, clear sight and heal those sores.
      That heart which lost, time brings from out the haze,
      and fervent prayers give thanks for end of wars.

    "No Man Gives Greater"

    [~pre-1944]
          Alone no one else but I
      To oversee bleak, torn earth.
      bald cold faced moon standing by
      counting war's desolate worth.

      Extreme the price! You may see
      At base of hill those white boards
      Plain, o'er men including me
      Who harmonized freedom's chords.

      Yet, here I remain alone
      To think, - seek to understand:
      Still within has no light shone,
      Gestures no revealing hand.

      It may mean, perhaps no rest
      For me who gave all I could.
      Those others, too,- what cruel jest
      Was flung neath ominous hood?

      Pay to dance, why pay we self,
      The cost then to us so high,
      Is there not a meaner pelf
      Mayhap liberty will buy?

      History's tongue gives answer, "no!"
      Through ages of tyrants laws,
      Greatest gave all to o'er throw; -
      We too helped the people's cause!

    Peanut Butter Buster

    A lively adventure by Alden W. Kelly

    It was with a lively feeling of adventure that Peanut Butter Buster took his first look at the world. Strange, he thought that of all my family I am the first one to be able to peep out of the jar in which we are confined as Peanut Butter, and know that I will soon step out on the pantry shelf, the first live Peanut Butter Boy in the whole world. As he sat there with just his eyes and strange hat showing over the edge of the Peanut Butter jar, his body was forming down below. You see, usually it takes too long for a Peanut Butter Person to form, but in this case with the lid off the jar, and the jar forgotten on the pantry shelf for a whole afternoon, Buster had the chance he needed to become alive.

    Mother had opened the jar, and then instead of stirring the oil from the top into the Peanut Butter right away, had set the jar down to go and see what the mailman had left in the mailbox. Peanut Butter People are never born unless the oil isn't stirred into the Peanut Butter, because the oil becomes the blood and perspiration of Peanut Butter People. So, the conditions had been just right for Buster.

    How did he get the name Buster? Why, as soon as he looked over the edge of the jar, it seemed to him as though he was Buster, and when a name just comes like that it sticks.

    Buster wiggled some to get his shoulders out of the jar, wiggled some more to get a fat stomach out, and at last reached one foot gingerly down to the shelf. Then as he got both feet down together and let go of the jar he fell down, BUMP. It wasn't because he couldn't walk, but because he'd been working so hard getting out of the jar that he'd been sweating, and the perspiration of Peanut Butter people is the oil from the top of the jar, and oil being slippery he'd quite naturally fallen down.

    Carefully he stood up in all his Peanut Butter glory. A flat 5 cornered hat cocked over one eye, Bright brown eyes, a round nose with speckles like a peanut shell, short chubby legs and a very round stomach, his clothes a Peanut Butter colored tight fitting suit. He looked just exactly the way he had imagined he would while in the jar. Then he said, "Hello", and not quite satisfied he said, "Hello, Buster."

    Hearing his own name almost made him fall down again because it seemed as though someone else was talking to him, but then he remembered that he had been talking in a chirrupy voice like a song-sparrow using words. "Why I'm not scared, that's me," he said, and then he heard mother's steps as she came towards the pantry. "oh dear", he chirruped, "I've got to hide, and if there's one thing I hate to do it's hide, because here I've just been born and I want the world to know it."

    But hide he did under a paper towel that happened to be on the shelf. Then mother hurried into the pantry saying "I've just time to make peanut butter sandwiches for the children when they get home from school", when she stopped in great surprise at seeing the peanut butter jar as empty as an egg shell that's been used.

    "What could have happened to the peanut butter?" she said, "Could it have spilled? No, it's not on the floor, but what is that grease under the paper towel?" and quickly lifting the towel, she exposed Buster. He had forgotten that he was still perspiring, and had soaked the towel.

    To say that mother was surprised is to not do Buster full justice, While she stood there unable to say a word, Buster stood up to his full height of six and inches and said, "Hello mother, I'm Buster, Peanut Butter Buster. I was just born, and now I'm going to find my fortune." He then jumped nimbly down to the pantry floor, skipped past mother, ran through the kitchen and out the back door which was just opening.

    He almost collided with Sonny and Sister who were just getting home from school, then with a chirrupy "good-by", he ran under the fence while the startled cries of the children made him run even faster.

    It happened that the path he had chosen lay between the house and the garage next door, and as he ran breathlessly around the corner of the garage, he dashed full force into Tabby, Sonny's kitten. She meowed in startled fashion, humped her back and was just in the act of jumping at Buster when she sniffed, took two hops backwards, turned one somersault and began purring. How Tabby liked peanut butter! Buster knew that if he was going to make his fortune he'd better hurry before Tabby got a lick at him, so off he went at a great speed and disappeared before Tabby knew that he was gone.

    He ran for a long time, because he was so afraid that Tabby might catch him, but then he got tired and his feet started to drag, and finally he just couldn't keep going so he sat down to rest. Then, Jocko Blue Jay began to make fun of Buster as only Jocko can. Buster couldn't understand what Jocko was saying, but it sounded so bad that Buster began to cry. Then, as Jocko continued to make fun of poor Buster, the other animals in the neighborhood came to see what was so funny. Fido, the Boston Bull came bounding and barking, but Buster was so sad that Fido sat down with him and had to dry his own eyes once or twice with his stump of a tail. Roberta, the Robin came too, and the sight of Buster sitting there almost made Roberta cry too, so she did the next best thing and cradled Buster's head with her wing. Seeing how sad Buster really was, Jocko began to feel sad and finally he flew down out of the tree and told Buster. He was sorry and stroked his back to make him feel better.

    Of course Buster was grateful for all the sympathy he was Jocko getting, but he finally told Fido and Roberat and Jocko that what he really wanted was a brother, and since he was a Peanut Butter Boy that was impossible. Then he slowly got to his feet and limped down the road out of sight.

    Fido, Roberta and Jocko felt so sad and talked about Buster for a while, then they all went to talk to Tabby and then the four of them talked to Sonny. Sonny talked to mother and mother sent Sonny to the store to buy another jar of peanut butter. Now if Buster comes back, Sonny is going to give him a Peanut Butter brother by leaving the top off the jar and not stirring the oil. However the whole neighborhood has been looking for Buster for two weeks! I think unless he is living with you, I think he’s gone forever. If he is… send him home right now.

    THE END
